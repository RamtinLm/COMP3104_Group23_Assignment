The software development lifecycle (SDLC) is the process teams follow to turn an idea into working software. It usually includes planning, requirements analysis, design, development, testing, deployment and maintenance. In traditional models such as Waterfall, each phase is finished before the next one starts, and development and operations are often handled by separate teams. This can make releases slow, and problems are often found late, when they are more expensive to fix.

DevOps changes this by connecting the phases into one continuous loop. With continuous integration, developers merge small changes often, and every change is built and tested automatically. Continuous delivery and deployment then move tested code to production quickly and safely. I saw a small version of this in our group assignment, where GitHub Actions ran a check every time one of us pushed or opened a pull request. The main benefits are faster releases, fewer bugs reaching users, quicker feedback, and better collaboration between developers and operations, which is why DevOps has become such an important part of the modern SDLC.
